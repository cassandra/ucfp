Methodology

Values. Totals are the "raw" benefit dollars received over a lifetime (grown by the SS adjustment). Present value restates that in today’s dollars, discounting each future year at inflation.{% if is_opportunity_cost %} Effective value discounts instead at your expected asset return ({{ real_return_pct }} above inflation), so a deferred benefit is also weighed against the growth that money could otherwise earn: the opportunity cost of waiting, and the figure strategies are ranked by.{% endif %}

{% if life_expectancy_estimated %}

Life expectancy. Each future year is weighted by your chance of still being alive to collect it, from the SSA period life table (ssa.gov) (cross-checked against CDC/NCHS). The ranked figures average over the lifespans you might have — so they are expected values that no single lifetime lands on exactly; the year-by-year above instead traces one representative lifetime, each person to about their life expectancy. It uses today’s mortality rates, so it runs slightly conservative on longevity{% if is_couple %}, treats the two lifespans as independent, and pays the survivor the deceased’s claim-age benefit{% endif %}. Shorter / Longer shifts the estimate a few years.

{% endif %}

SS adjustment. The Social Security cost-of-living adjustment is modeled at about {{ cola_lag_pct }} below inflation, its long-run pattern, so benefits keep most, but not quite all, of their purchasing power over time. Set the inflation rate to match how fast you expect prices to rise; the adjustment follows from it.
