Total is the benefit dollars received over a lifetime (grown by the SS adjustment). Present value restates that in today’s dollars, discounting each future year at inflation.{% if is_opportunity_cost %} Effective value discounts instead at your expected asset return ({{ real_return_pct }} above inflation), so a deferred benefit is also weighed against the growth that money could otherwise earn — the opportunity cost of waiting, and the figure strategies are ranked by.{% endif %}{% if life_expectancy_estimated %} Every figure above is an expected value — each future year weighted by the chance you’re alive to receive it, then summed — so no single lifetime produces exactly these totals. The year-by-year below shows one representative lifetime instead.{% endif %}

The Social Security cost-of-living adjustment (the “SS adjustment” above) is modeled at about {{ cola_lag_pct }} below inflation, its long-run pattern — so benefits keep most, but not quite all, of their purchasing power over time. Set the inflation rate to match how fast you expect prices to rise; the adjustment follows from it.
